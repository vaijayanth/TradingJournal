Init Risk % Distribution
Are your stops consistently sized or all over the place?
What to look for: The bulk of bars should land in the 1–2% risk zone. If they're spread across all buckets you're not sizing consistently — each trade carries different risk which makes your system hard to evaluate.
Action: If you see many trades >3% risk, those stops are too wide — you're giving the stock too much room and risking too much capital. If most are <1%, stops may be too tight and getting hit by normal noise.
Win Rate by Risk Size
Do tighter or wider stops produce better outcomes?
What to look for: If tighter stops (<1%) win less often, they're too close to price noise and getting triggered prematurely. If wider stops (>3%) win more but your losses are big, you're trading win rate for pain.
Action: Find the risk bucket with the best win rate — that's your edge zone. Move your stop placement towards that range. Ideally 1–2% risk should have the highest or equal win rate.
Winner Size Distribution
Where do winners close? — calibrates when to move stop to breakeven
What to look for: If most winners cluster in 0–2%, your winners are tiny — exits are too early or setups aren't running. You want a right-skewed shape: most bars in 2–10%+ with a long tail.
Action: The BE stop zone annotation marks 2–5%. If >70% of your winners are above that line, moving stop to breakeven at +2% profit is safe. If most winners close below it, don't add a breakeven rule yet — you'll stop yourself out of winners.
Loss Size Distribution
Are losses clean (near init risk) or messy (overrun)?
What to look for: Losses should cluster tightly around your avg init risk bucket (marked in amber). If you see a large spike in 5–8% or >8%, those are uncontrolled losses — stops moved, gap-downs, or held through the stop.
Action: Any loss more than 2× your init risk is a process failure, not a market failure. Count them. If >10% of your losses are outsized, that's the single biggest lever to fix — one big loss can wipe 5–10 normal stops.
Pattern Analysis
Trade Age vs P&L%
Hold time vs outcome — are you exiting too early?
What to look for: Green dots (winners) should cluster in the top-right — held longer, bigger gain. If your biggest winners are all held <10 days, you're exiting too early. Red dots (losers) should cluster bottom-left — stopped quickly.
Action: If you see green dots scattered left (short hold, good gain) that's fine — quick winners happen. But if most big winners are also short-held, ask if you're taking profit too early out of fear. The goal: let green run, cut red fast.
Entry Velocity
New positions per week — primary overtrading detector
What to look for: Weeks with >3 new entries are a warning sign. Overtrading usually happens after a loss streak (revenge trading) or a big win (overconfidence). The amber line marks the threshold.
Action: Find your worst-performing weeks and cross-check with entry velocity spikes — they usually correlate. Consider a hard rule: max 2–3 new positions per week. Sitting in cash is also a position.
