# Bonde's own rules rewrite the EP playbook

Pradeep Bonde's own blog posts, read in full this time, show that his Episodic Pivot (EP) is a close-to-close volume-surge scan plus a judgment about the catalyst. It is not the "gap-and-opening-range-break" setup that third-party summaries describe. His 2010 scan flags a stock up at least 8% on volume at least 3x its 100-day average, and his 2014 version flags 4%+ on 3x the 50-day average with at least 300,000 shares, and neither formula contains a gap term. He buys immediately, often in pre-market, and the one stop convention he states (the day's low) comes from a generic risk post, not an EP-specific rule. He found no EP trailing-average exit: his documented exit is profit-taking in quarters toward a 40-50% target. Mark Minervini's side of the ledger is still second-hand, because every Minervini and independent-evidence host stayed unreachable, so his Trend Template, VCP, pivot and stop numbers remain **unverified community or secondary figures**. For a swing system that buys a 252-day closing high with Supertrend green on Indian equities, the mechanically codable pieces are the Trend Template's price and moving-average tests, Bonde's close-to-close volume-surge flag, a Momentum Burst flag and a 4%-range-expansion test. The catalyst, neglect assessment, earnings acceleration and VCP shape are not directly codable from price and volume, and none has independent backtest evidence. Your own backtest remains the only test that counts.

## What changed from the first report

Several third-party claims in the earlier report fail against Bonde's own text. The "8-10%+ gap" is wrong as a definition, since both of his formulas are close over prior close and a gap is never mentioned, and his 2010 study of 25%-per-month movers says "stocks with gaps below 10% or no gap are better" and that "large gaps are not good" ([Stockbee, Nov 2010](https://stockbee.blogspot.com/2010/11/what-makes-stock-go-up-25-plus-in-month.html)). The "wait 5-30 minutes and buy the opening-range break" entry is also not Bonde's: he writes that "if I find a good EP I buy immediately in pre or post market" and that most of his big EP wins were bought pre-market ([Stockbee, Jul 2014](https://stockbee.blogspot.com/2014/07/my-process-flow-for-episodic-pivots-ep.html)). The "10-day MA trail" and any EP-specific moving-average exit were not found in any Bonde post. His risk statements conflict across time: a 2007 reader answer says he risks 5-10% on EP ideas and about 1% on EPS-momentum breakouts ([Stockbee, 2007](https://stockbee.blogspot.com/2007/03/reader-questions.html)), while a 2014 post says 0.25-4% per trade, mostly 0.25-1% ([Stockbee, Aug 2014](https://stockbee.blogspot.com/2014/08/how-i-control-my-risk.html)). The frequency claim also tightens, since he says "on an average I find one good trade like this in a year", with some years yielding 10-12 and some only 1-2 ([Stockbee, Jul 2014](https://stockbee.blogspot.com/2014/07/few-good-earnings-trade-can-change-your.html)). Confirmed from the old report: the 8% / 3x / 100-day-average core of the 2010 scan, and the 3-5 day Momentum Burst. New: the volume floors in the scan, the neglect definition, the catalyst taxonomy and the 40-50% EP profit target.

## Bonde's EP and Momentum Burst, as he wrote them

The verbatim 2010 Telechart scan is an OR of two branches. Branch A is up at least $5 with volume above 10,000, price at least 62.50 and volume above the prior day. Branch B is up at least 8%, volume above 3,000, volume at least 300% of the 100-day average and price above 1 ([Stockbee, Feb 2010](https://stockbee.blogspot.com/2010/02/what-are-episodic-pivots-and-how-to.html)). The unit of the volume floors (shares or hundreds) is not stated, so those absolute numbers are **unconfirmed**. He describes the scan as looking for "an out-sized price move on high volume post earnings", and says big earnings breakouts typically show volume of 10x or more of average. He prefers floats under 25 million, with the best moves under 10 million, and is unenthusiastic about floats above 500 million. The 2014 intraday version is "c/c1>1.04 and v>3*avgv50.1 and v>=300000", run repeatedly during the day, with the real test being "neglect + game changing earnings" ([Stockbee, Jul 2014](https://stockbee.blogspot.com/2014/07/my-process-flow-for-episodic-pivots-ep.html)). He also says pre-market stocks up on under 50k volume, and large caps up on 50k volume, are not worth attention ([Stockbee, Sep 2014](https://stockbee.blogspot.com/2014/09/everyday-hunt-for-stocks-with-big.html)). The Telechart floors and the 300,000 floor are US-share numbers, so Indian floors would have to be set by you.

The judgment layer is where Bonde puts the weight. He calls prior neglect "the single most important criteria" and defines it five ways: no earnings growth for a long time, no meaningful price rally, below-average volume, no analyst coverage or news, and low fund ownership ([Stockbee, Nov 2010](https://stockbee.blogspot.com/2010/11/what-makes-stock-go-up-25-plus-in-month.html)). He wants the first or second big earnings surprise, calling third and fourth surprises "priced in" or prone to gap-and-reverse ([Stockbee, Sep 2013](https://stockbee.blogspot.com/2013/09/night-time-is-right-time.html)). He lists roughly 20 catalyst types, from earnings acceleration of 100%+ through contracts, drug approvals, buyouts and regulatory changes to media mentions, but advises concentrating on earnings because they are easier to understand ([Stockbee, Feb 2010](https://stockbee.blogspot.com/2010/02/what-are-episodic-pivots-and-how-to.html)). No numeric lookback for "months or years" of neglect exists in his text. His delayed-reaction EP rules were not found: the May 2024 post is only an embedded video ([Stockbee, May 2024](https://stockbee.blogspot.com/2024/05/delayed-reaction-episodic-pivots.html)).

On risk and exits, his documented EP conventions are sparse and partly generic. Position size is risk divided by entry minus stop, with the day's low as the stop in his worked examples and a 1%-risk sizing formula that "assumes your stop is low of the day" ([Stockbee, Aug 2014](https://stockbee.blogspot.com/2014/08/how-i-control-my-risk.html)). In a 2011 interview he says he targets 8-20% on swings and 40-50% on EP trades, sells in quarters, takes 50-75% off into a 10%+ move, and exits after 3 days if the trade has not moved ([Stockbee, Jun 2011](https://stockbee.blogspot.com/2011/06/q-with-pradeep-bonde.html)). He says "14 big trades like these have contributed to 80% of my profit in last 14 years", a fat-tailed profile ([Stockbee, Sep 2014](https://stockbee.blogspot.com/2014/09/everyday-hunt-for-stocks-with-big.html)). His only stated win rate is "60% of trades are positive" for his 2014 trading overall, not EP alone, and no EP-specific win rate exists in his words. He also notes that trend-following methods typically succeed on 20-25% of trades ([Stockbee, Dec 2010](https://stockbee.blogspot.com/2010/12/exits-are-inherent-to-setups-part1.html)).

The Momentum Burst is a separate, catalyst-free setup. His scan is "c/c1>=1.04 and v>v1 and v>=100000", and he warns that it "throws a wide net": filters (not up three days in a row, a narrow-range or down day before, a smooth prior move, a young trend with first or second breakouts) eliminate over 95% of hits ([Stockbee, May 2015](https://stockbee.blogspot.com/2015/05/how-do-stock-move-on-3-to-5-day-time.html)). A burst lasts 3-5 days, gains 8-20% (up to 40% on cheap stocks), and holding longer gives the gains back ([Stockbee, Jan 2014](https://stockbee.blogspot.com/2014/01/swing-trading-using-momentum-bursts.html)). He also says to avoid breakout trades near market turns when many breakouts fail. His quality guidelines (range expansion day, volume above the prior day, a narrow-range day before, an orderly prior uptrend, a close near the high) are qualitative, with no numeric definitions of "narrow range" or "close near high" ([Stockbee, Jul 2016](https://stockbee.blogspot.com/2016/07/guidelines-to-find-good-momentum-burst.html)).

## Minervini's entry stack remains secondary-sourced

None of the following was read from Minervini's books or site, so every figure here is **unverified** against him. The Trend Template is a screen, not a trigger. Secondary sources agree on the moving-average structure: price above the 150- and 200-day averages, the 150 above the 200, the 50 above both and the price above the 50, and a 200-day average rising for at least a month (one open-source script uses 22 trading days) ([ChartMill](https://www.chartmill.com/documentation/stock-screener/technical-analysis-trading-strategies/496-Mark-Minervini-Trend-Template-A-Step-by-Step-Guide-for-Beginners), [TradingView script](https://www.tradingview.com/script/zygjiw4C-Minervini-Trend-Template)). Price must sit within 25% of its 52-week high and have a relative-strength rank of at least 70 ([ChartMill](https://www.chartmill.com/trading-ideas/645-Mark-Minervinis-Trend-Template-TTP)). The distance above the 52-week low is disputed between 25% and 30%. An MQL5 article attributes 25% to "Think and Trade Like a Champion" and 30% to an earlier book, but this was seen only in a search summary ([MQL5](https://www.mql5.com/en/articles/22790)). Some Indian screeners drop the RS rule altogether ([Trendlyne](https://trendlyne.com/fundamentals/stock-screener-history/401689)).

The Volatility Contraction Pattern (VCP) is described as a Stage 2 base with typically 2-4 progressively shallower pullbacks and drying volume, ending at a pivot that is the high of the last contraction ([ChartMill VCP](https://www.chartmill.com/documentation/technical-analysis/chart-patterns/531-Volatility-Contraction-Pattern-VCP-How-to-Spot-and-Trade-It), [ChartMill VCP guide](https://www.chartmill.com/images/uploads/The-Complete-ChartMill-VCP-Guide.pdf)). Breakout volume 40-50% above average, a buy zone up to 5% above the pivot and the "25-12-6" depth sequence are community conventions that no source ties to Minervini's own text. His cheat, low-cheat, Power Play and Code 33 variants have only qualitative descriptions, without numbers ([Setup Factory](https://thesetupfactory.substack.com/p/mastering-the-minervini-low-cheat)). Risk figures found are a 7-8% maximum loss and 1.25-2.5% of equity at risk per trade ([ChartMill](https://www.chartmill.com/documentation/trading-and-investing/methodologies/524-Sell-Rules-for-Growth-Stocks-When-to-Get-Out), [TraderLion](https://traderlion.com/investing-champions/mark-minervinis-risk-management/)). The 3:1 target, a roughly 50% win rate and a 220% average annual return were not confirmed. The one verified performance number is his **+334.8% in the 2021 US Investing Championship**, a self-selected contest result and not an audited strategy test ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20220124005241/en/2021-United-States-Investing-Championship-Winners-%E2%80%94-Minervini-Smashes-Record)).

## How the setups compare, and what the evidence supports

The two traders buy different things. Bonde's EP buys a catalyst-driven volume surge in a neglected, often low-float stock on the first day, with a small stop and a fat-tailed payoff. His Momentum Burst buys a 4% range expansion with no catalyst for 3-5 days. Minervini buys a stock already in a confirmed Stage 2 uptrend near its 52-week high at a low-risk pivot after contraction. The sharpest contrast is neglect: Bonde's premium is on stocks with no prior rally, while the Trend Template demands a stock already up at least 25-30% off its low and near its high, so the two screens select nearly opposite pre-event profiles. Your system sits closer to Minervini on that axis, since a 252-day closing high is strength by construction, but it shares Bonde's no-opinion-on-fundamentals, mechanical character.

| Dimension | Bonde EP | Bonde Momentum Burst | Minervini Template + VCP | Your system |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Trigger | Close up 8%+ on 3x 100-day volume (2010); 4%+ on 3x 50-day volume, 300k (2014) | Close up 4%+, volume above prior day, 100k floor | Break of the last-contraction pivot (community definition) | New 252-session closing high, Supertrend green |
| Non-price input | Catalyst, neglect, float | None | RS rank; optional fundamentals | None |
| Stop | Day's low in worked examples (not EP-specific) | Not documented | 7-8% maximum (unverified) | None; exit on Supertrend red |
| Exit | 40-50% target, sell in quarters, 3-day time exit | 3-5 days | Not verified | Supertrend red |
| Independent evidence | None found | None found | Vendor tests only (unverified) | Own 10-year backtest |

The independent evidence is thin and partly unverified. George and Hwang (2004) find that nearness to the 52-week high predicts returns better than past returns, with a roughly 0.45% monthly profit that is **unverified in magnitude** ([paper](https://www.bauer.uh.edu/tgeorge/papers/gh5-paper.pdf), [summary](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/361244)). International replications and Indian momentum studies exist but are monthly-portfolio tests at 3-12 month horizons ([NAU](https://experts.nau.edu/en/publications/the-52-week-high-and-momentum-investing-in-international-stock-in/), [NSE paper](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/research/content/RP_2_Feb2012.pdf)). EP's thesis rests on post-earnings-announcement drift, which Martineau finds gone for non-microcaps by about 2006, while a 2025 summary reports other papers still finding it ([Martineau](https://cfr.ivo-welch.info/published/papers/martineau2021rest.pdf), [UCLA Anderson Review](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/is-post-earnings-announcement-drift-a-thing-again)). Bonde himself says momentum-burst timing is a judgment call that is hard to backtest ([Retail Traders Repository](https://retailtradersrepository.substack.com/p/pradeep-bonde-episodic-pivots)). The MarketInOut Trend Template backtests and the Stonks Capital EP test could not be opened in either pass, so they stay **unverified hypotheses**, not evidence ([MarketInOut](https://www.marketinout.com/blog/minervini-trend-template-backtest-markets), [Stonks Capital](https://stonkscapital.substack.com/p/modeling-kullamagi-part-3-episodic)). A Nifty 500 Supertrend test over 2012-2025 shows about 6 in 10 trades losing ([Share.Market](https://www.share.market/buzz/insights/i-backtested-200000-trades-using-the-supertrend-indicator-here-is-what-actually-works)), which is consistent with the roughly 41-42% win rate in your repo's own backtests (ND profit factor about 2.0, AZ about 1.75, per `swing-backtest/RESULTS.md`).

## What is mechanically codable for a 252-day-high plus Supertrend system

From daily OHLCV, the following can be coded exactly or nearly: the Trend Template's moving-average ordering and price bounds (with a 200-day slope lookback of your choosing), a within-25%-of-high test (nearly always true for a new 252-day closing high), a universe-relative RS percentile, Bonde's close-to-close surge flag (close over prior close of 1.08 and volume at least 3x its 100-day average, or 1.04 and 3x its 50-day average), his Momentum Burst flag (1.04, volume above the prior day, a volume floor), a not-up-three-days-in-a-row test and a prior-day-range-versus-recent-ranges test as a "narrow range" proxy. Bonde's rules are close-to-close, so a daily-bar backtest of them is truer to his scan than any opening-range or gap logic. The catalyst, earnings acceleration, surprise count, neglect, float and analyst coverage are not in price data, and delayed-reaction EP is undocumented. A VCP can only be approximated, with every depth, count and tightness threshold a free parameter you would be inventing. Indian floors and NSE price bands matter at the surge: bands run 2-20% with F&O stocks on a dynamic 10% band ([NSE](https://www.nseindia.com/static/products-services/equity-market-price-bands)), so a stock locked at upper circuit on results day cannot be bought at the gap. That is my inference, and no source quantifies it.

Fit with your system is mixed. A new 252-day closing high combined with a volume-surge day is the overlap with Bonde's flag, and with Supertrend green already on, a 3x-volume 8% day is likely to coincide with your signal on some names and could be tested as a tag. The pure EP idea (neglected, low-float, pre-market entry, 40-50% targets, fat-tailed 1-in-a-year trades) conflicts with your design on three counts: no stops, no profit targets and a Nifty F&O or large-liquid universe where low-float neglect is scarce. The cheapest tests, all my suggestions, are: (1) a Trend Template overlay on the ND universe; (2) a minimum-volume-multiple filter on the signal day; (3) a subset tagged by Bonde's 8%-on-3x surge, compared with untagged signals; (4) an earnings-window flag from the Yahoo results dates already in the repo; (5) a Momentum Burst side-sleeve with a 3-5 day exit, tested separately because it has a different holding period and no trailing exit. Reject any rule needing a hard 7-8% stop or an EP day's-low stop, since those belong to different strategies from yours.

## Conclusion

The key change is that primary reading made Bonde's EP both simpler and less portable than the earlier report implied. The scan is a plain close-to-close surge, and the edge he describes lives in a hand-judged layer (neglect, first surprise, low float) that no OHLCV test can reproduce, which is also why no one has produced a clean independent backtest of it. The Minervini half is the reverse: the filters are easy to code, but their numbers are still community-sourced and not checked against his books.

What remains to verify is concrete. Minervini's own text for the eight Trend Template criteria (25% versus 30% above the low, the 200-day slope length), his VCP depth, volume and buy-range statements, his stop and win-rate claims, the MarketInOut and Stonks Capital test designs and results, TraderLion's Bonde pages (403 to the fetcher), the unit of the Telechart volume floors, Bonde's delayed-reaction rules and the George-Hwang and Martineau magnitudes. Until those are read, treat every Minervini and independent figure here as a hypothesis for your own backtest to confirm or reject.
