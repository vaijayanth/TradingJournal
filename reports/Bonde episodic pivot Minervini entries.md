# Decoding Bonde and Minervini for NSE

**Bottom line: only the Trend Template, a 4% range-expansion scan and a gap-plus-volume proxy for Episodic Pivots can be coded from daily price and volume, and none of the three has independent evidence of an edge.** Pradeep Bonde's Episodic Pivot (EP) is a catalyst trade (a large gap, a volume surge and usually an earnings or news event). Its best-sourced scan is roughly an 8% gain on about 3x average volume, but the catalyst itself is not in price data. Bonde's related Momentum Burst is a 4%+ range expansion that he says runs 3 to 5 days. Mark Minervini's entry stack is the 8-rule Trend Template (a screen), the Volatility Contraction Pattern (VCP) pivot (the trigger) and a 7-8% maximum stop. Of the three, the Trend Template is the most codable, and the VCP only approximately. The strongest academic support found is for your own signal family, proximity to the 52-week high combined with trend. No independent backtest of EP, Momentum Burst or VCP turned up. For your system (252-day closing high plus Supertrend green, no stop) the realistic carry-overs are liquidity and volume filters, a Trend Template overlay, an optional gap-volume flag and an earnings-calendar flag, each to be tested against your own backtest rather than adopted on trust.

Stockbee's blog, TraderLion, Substack, Medium, MarketInOut, TradingQnA and Stonks Capital could not be fetched. Minervini's books and site were never reached. Bonde's paid material was inaccessible. ## Bonde's Episodic Pivot is a catalyst gap, and its rules are thinly documented

The best-sourced EP definition is a scan in Bonde's February 2010 Stockbee post: close up at least 8% on the prior close, volume at least 3x its 100-day average, and a minimum-volume floor whose value was not captured **[unverified: exact formula]** ([Stockbee 2010](https://stockbee.blogspot.com/2010/02/what-are-episodic-pivots-and-how-to.html?m=0)). Third-party write-ups loosen this to an 8-10%+ gap, a 2-4x+ volume multiple, a catalyst and a strong close ([Tradingsim](https://tradingsim.com/blog/episodic-pivot-power-earnings-gap-buyable-gap-up-explained/), [TheRightTrader](https://therighttrader.com/learn/trading-strategies/episodic-pivot)). A protected TradingView script based on Bonde uses a 10%+ gap on 2x+ volume ([TradingView](https://il.tradingview.com/script/njlDpagc-Pradeep-Bonde-Stockbee-Momentum-Pro)). So 8% / 3x / 100-day average is the best-sourced baseline, not a confirmed final rule. I found nothing documenting a price floor, a dollar-volume floor or a neglected-stock (no prior run-up) criterion, and no Bonde-worded catalyst taxonomy. TraderLion's Bonde material frames EP as catalyst-driven and stresses market conditions and the stock's character ([TraderLion](https://traderlion.com/profile/pradeep-bonde/episodic-pivots/)).

Entry mechanics are consistently described by third parties as waiting roughly 5-30 minutes after the open, marking the opening range and buying a break of its high, with the stop at the low of day (some say the opening-range low) ([Tradezella](https://www.tradezella.com/strategies/episode-pivot-strategy), [TheRightTrader](https://therighttrader.com/learn/trading-strategies/episodic-pivot)). It is unresolved which of these Bonde himself treats as primary. His "Delayed Reaction EP" waits for a secondary move after digestion, either a red-to-green day or a breakout from a tight range after the catalyst day, to avoid paying inflated day-one prices ([TraderLion](https://traderlion.com/profile/pradeep-bonde/episodic-pivots-delayed-reaction/)). The sizing guidance found is generic fixed-fractional (a fixed share of equity divided by the distance to the gap-day low), not a documented Bonde rule ([Finer Market Points](https://www.finermarketpoints.com/post/episodic-pivot-trading-complete-guide)).

Exits are the weakest-documented area. Bonde's Momentum Burst material says bursts last 3-5 days and gain typically 8-20%, up to 40% in lower-priced stocks, after which holding gives gains back ([Stockbee 2013](https://stockbee.blogspot.com/2013/12/stocks-move-in-short-term-momentum.html), [Stockbee 2015](https://stockbee.blogspot.com/2015/05/how-do-stock-move-on-3-to-5-day-time.html)). Exit rules that appear (first down day after 3+ up days, close below the 5-day MA, trail under the latest pullback low after +2R, close below the gap-day open) come from third-party scripts and pages, not from Bonde ([TradingView](https://il.tradingview.com/script/njlDpagc-Pradeep-Bonde-Stockbee-Momentum-Pro), [TheRightTrader](https://therighttrader.com/learn/trading-strategies/episodic-pivot)). A 10-day MA trail was not found in any Bonde source. Win rates are equally murky. One blog cites 60-70% in favourable markets, attributed to secondary analysis, while another guide says no reliable published figure exists **[both unverified]** ([Finer Market Points](https://www.finermarketpoints.com/post/episodic-pivot-trading-complete-guide), [TheRightTrader](https://therighttrader.com/learn/trading-strategies/episodic-pivot)).

The related Stockbee scans are better specified but vary by transcription. The core Momentum Burst is a 4% breakout on volume above the preceding 8-10 days, usually after a narrow-range day ([Stockbee 2014](https://stockbee.blogspot.com/2014/01/swing-trading-using-momentum-bursts.html)). Later guidelines add a close at or near the high, a stock that is not extended, and the first or second breakout early in an uptrend ([Stockbee 2016](https://stockbee.blogspot.com/2016/07/guidelines-to-find-good-momentum-burst.html)). One transcription codes it as close over prior close above 1.04 with price and volume floors whose values were not captured **[unverified]** ([Retail Traders Repository](https://retailtradersrepository.substack.com/p/pradeep-bonde-momentum-bursts)). Third-party TradingView versions use volume above the previous bar, a 4% gain and a close in the top 30% of the range, some with a 100,000-share floor ([TradingView](https://in.tradingview.com/script/YNH3Dy2j-StockBee-4-Breakout)). "Anticipation" setups buy a quiet 3-20 day base before the expansion day. The practical distinction is that EP needs an event and a wide first day, while a burst or anticipation setup needs a pre-breakout contraction.

## Minervini's stack is a trend screen, a pattern pivot and a tight stop

The Trend Template is a screen, not a buy signal ([TradingView SEPA System](https://in.tradingview.com/script/kcihs4Hp-Minervini-SEPA-System)). Secondary sources agree on the moving-average structure: price above the 150- and 200-day MAs, the 150 above the 200, the 50 above both, price above the 50, and the 200-day MA rising for at least a month (one open-source implementation uses 22 trading days) ([ChartMill](https://www.chartmill.com/documentation/stock-screener/technical-analysis-trading-strategies/496-Mark-Minervini-Trend-Template-A-Step-by-Step-Guide-for-Beginners), [TradingView script](https://www.tradingview.com/script/zygjiw4C-Minervini-Trend-Template)). Price must be within 25% of its 52-week high ([ChartMill](https://www.chartmill.com/trading-ideas/645-Mark-Minervinis-Trend-Template-TTP)) and have an RS rank of at least 70, ideally 80-90+ ([ChartMill](https://www.chartmill.com/documentation/stock-screener/technical-analysis-trading-strategies/496-Mark-Minervini-Trend-Template-A-Step-by-Step-Guide-for-Beginners)). The distance above the 52-week low is disputed: 30% in ChartMill and 25% in code, with one source attributing 25% to one Minervini book and 30% to another **[unverified against the books]**. The exact RS formula and the 200-day slope threshold in his own text are not confirmed. Some Indian screeners drop the RS rule entirely ([Trendlyne](https://trendlyne.com/fundamentals/stock-screener-history/401689)).

The VCP is a Stage 2 base with typically 2-4 progressively shallower pullbacks and drying volume, ending in a tight pivot ([ChartMill](https://www.chartmill.com/documentation/technical-analysis/chart-patterns/531-Volatility-Contraction-Pattern-VCP-How-to-Spot-and-Trade-It), [Trade Ideas](https://www.trade-ideas.com/learning-center/chart-patterns/volatility-contraction-pattern-vcp-explained/)). The pivot is the high of the final contraction, not necessarily the base high ([ChartMill VCP guide](https://www.chartmill.com/images/uploads/The-Complete-ChartMill-VCP-Guide.pdf)). Sources cite breakout volume at least 40-50% above the 50-day average, a buy zone from the pivot to 5% above it, and no chasing beyond 10% above, but the last two are from a community file, not Minervini **[unverified]** ([ChartMill](https://www.chartmill.com/documentation/technical-analysis/chart-patterns/531-Volatility-Contraction-Pattern-VCP-How-to-Spot-and-Trade-It), [skills.sh](https://skills.sh/copyleftdev/sk1llz/minervini-swing-trading)). The depth sequences (that file gives 20-35%, then 10-20%, 5-15%, 3-8%) and the popular "25-12-6" sequence are conventions that no source documents as Minervini's rules. No numeric definition of base length or tightness was found. Variants (low cheat, Power Play, Code 33 earnings acceleration) have only thin qualitative descriptions, with no numbers ([Setup Factory](https://thesetupfactory.substack.com/p/mastering-the-minervini-low-cheat), [TradingView Code 33](https://www.tradingview.com/script/z8bo5R1T-Minervini-Code-33-Matrix/)).

Risk rules found are a maximum loss of about 7-8%, often tighter ([ChartMill](https://www.chartmill.com/documentation/trading-and-investing/methodologies/524-Sell-Rules-for-Growth-Stocks-When-to-Get-Out)), 1.25-2.5% of equity at risk per trade ([TraderLion](https://traderlion.com/investing-champions/mark-minervinis-risk-management/)), and pyramiding only into winners with decreasing add sizes ([Tintin Trading](https://tintintrading.substack.com/p/maximizing-profits-with-progressive?open=false)). The 3:1 reward-risk target, the "never let a good profit turn into a loss" wording, a win rate near 50% and a 220% average annual return were not confirmed **[unverified]**. The one verified performance figure is **+334.8% in the 2021 US Investing Championship** stock division, against a prior record of +119.1% ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20220124005241/en/2021-United-States-Investing-Championship-Winners-%E2%80%94-Minervini-Smashes-Record)). That is a self-selected competition result, not an audited strategy test. No source tied his entries to a market-regime rule.

## Evidence favours your signal family over EP and VCP

The sharpest finding is how the evidence ranks. George and Hwang (2004) show nearness to the 52-week high predicts returns better than past returns and explains much of momentum profit, roughly 0.45% a month **[unverified magnitude]** ([summary](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/361244), [paper](https://www.bauer.uh.edu/tgeorge/papers/gh5-paper.pdf)). Replications report profits in 18 of 20 international markets, significant in 10, and 10 of 16 in another paper; the sources differ on the count and India was not clearly confirmed in the snippets ([NAU](https://experts.nau.edu/en/publications/the-52-week-high-and-momentum-investing-in-international-stock-in/)). Indian momentum studies support 3-12 month horizons: Ansari and Khan (1995-2006) and Joshipura (1995-2008) ([Emerald](https://emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/03074351211193730/full/html), [NSE paper](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/research/content/RP_2_Feb2012.pdf)). All of these are older, monthly-portfolio tests, so they support the signal family but not a daily-breakout-with-trailing-exit system, and none tests costs at your scale.

EP's thesis rests on post-earnings-announcement drift, which is contested. Martineau finds drift gone for non-microcaps by about 2006 ([Martineau](https://cfr.ivo-welch.info/published/papers/martineau2021rest.pdf)), while a 2025 summary reports other accepted papers finding it alive and attributes the dispute to research design ([UCLA Anderson Review](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/is-post-earnings-announcement-drift-a-thing-again)). No academic study isolates the actual EP trigger, a large earnings gap on huge volume, and none tests EP, Momentum Burst or VCP in India. The only public tests of the Trend Template are vendor backtests (MarketInOut), which found smaller drawdowns where it worked, warned that some edges may be chance, and found the exit rule drove results (a patient exit beat a TSX index fund by about 1.3 points a year) **[unverified: pages not fetched]** ([MarketInOut](https://www.marketinout.com/blog/minervini-trend-template-backtest-markets), [TSX](https://www.marketinout.com/blog/minervini-trend-template-backtest-tsx)). A generic buy-every-earnings-day test with a 20-SMA trail reportedly showed no edge, but it is not Bonde's EP **[unverified]** ([Stonks Capital](https://stonkscapital.substack.com/p/modeling-kullamagi-part-3-episodic)). Bonde's own material says when Momentum Burst works is a judgment call that is hard to backtest, and that EPs have been less frequent recently ([Retail Traders Repository](https://retailtradersrepository.substack.com/p/pradeep-bonde-episodic-pivots)). For the exit leg, a Nifty 500 Supertrend test over 2012-2025 shows about 6 in 10 trades losing ([Share.Market](https://www.share.market/buzz/insights/i-backtested-200000-trades-using-the-supertrend-indicator-here-is-what-actually-works)). That fits your 41-42% win rate with large average winners (from your repo's `swing-backtest/RESULTS.md`, ND profit factor about 2.0, AZ about 1.75). Your system looks like trend following, and your own backtest is more transparent than any public Minervini or Bonde test I found.

## How the four approaches compare, and what carries over

| Dimension | Episodic Pivot | Momentum Burst | Minervini Trend Template + VCP | Your system (252-day closing high + Supertrend green) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Trigger | Gap about 8-10%+ on 3x+ volume with a catalyst | Close 4%+ up on volume above prior day | Break of the final-contraction pivot | New 252-session closing high, Supertrend green, buy next session |
| Needs non-price data | Yes (catalyst, neglect) | No | Partly (RS rank; fundamentals optional) | No |
| Stop | Low of day (third-party) | Tight, discretionary | About 7-8% max | None; exit on Supertrend red |
| Hold | Days to weeks | 3-5 days (Bonde) | Weeks to months | Open-ended |
| Independent evidence | None found | None found | Vendor tests only | Your own 10-year backtest |

The comparison cells for EP and Burst stops and for Minervini's win rate come from general descriptions in the notes and are not sourced to the originators. Mechanically, a daily OHLCV feed supports the Trend Template (SMA ordering, 200-day slope, 52-week-high and -low bounds, universe-relative RS percentile), the 4% scan, gap percentage versus prior close, a volume multiple, a 52-week closing high and Supertrend. It cannot support EP's catalyst, EPS or revenue acceleration, surprise size or neglect. A gap of 8-10%+ on 3-5x volume is a usable proxy for a catalyst day (my suggestion, not tested). Your repo already fetches results and ex-dividend dates from Yahoo for options, which could flag earnings-driven gaps. VCP can only be approximated, for example as "each contraction shallower than the last, final range under a tightness cap, volume below its 50-day average", with every threshold left for you to tune.

Indian market structure bites at the gap. NSE price bands run 2%, 5%, 10% and 20%, with F&O stocks on a dynamic 10% band measured from the prior close ([NSE](https://www.nseindia.com/static/products-services/equity-market-price-bands)); a secondary report of mass band revisions in May 2026 is **[unverified]** ([Kotak Neo](https://www.kotakneo.com/news/market-news/nse-price-band-revision-stock-volatility-update/)). A stock locked at upper circuit on a results day cannot be bought at the gap, so EP-style entries in small band-limited names are partly unfillable. This is my inference, and no source quantifies it. Your next-session AMO limit (signal-day high capped at close +2%) already avoids chasing, and the AZ F&O universe bounds the issue.

## What to test, in order of payoff

The evidence suggests the exit matters more than the entry style (the MarketInOut exit finding is unverified, but it matches the Supertrend results above). Entry overlays should therefore be judged by whether they improve your existing backtest rather than by the reputation of the originator. The cheapest tests, all my suggestions, are: (1) a Trend Template overlay on the ND universe, noting that your current AZ and ND filters (200 and 50 EMA in SST) are looser than the template; (2) a liquidity and minimum-volume-multiple filter on the signal day; (3) a tagged subset where the signal day is also an 8%+, 3x-volume gap, compared against untagged signals; (4) an earnings-window flag from the Yahoo results dates already in the repo. Leave out of the system any rule that needs a 7-8% stop, since your design deliberately has none, and treat Minervini's stop-based expectancy as a different strategy.

## Conclusion

The sharpest takeaway is a mismatch between fame and evidence. The two named methods, EP and VCP, are the least mechanically testable and have no independent backtests found, while the unglamorous element your system already uses, proximity to a 52-week high plus trend, has peer-reviewed support, including Indian momentum studies. EP also depends on the most contested anomaly (post-earnings drift) and on non-price data you would have to source. That shifts the question from "which guru's entry should we copy" to "which of their filters survive in our own backtest".
